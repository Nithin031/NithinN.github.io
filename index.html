April 2026 – Present Mangalore, Karnataka Machine Learning Member & Advisor

Fracto AI

Computer Vision, Industrial AI

Fracto builds tools for industrial failure analysis. When a part breaks, its fracture surface and the machine’s sensor history both say something about why. The goal is a platform that reads both, finds the root cause, and flags the next failure before it happens. I work on the machine learning behind it and advise on research direction.

Most of the work starts with data. Sensor logs and inspection images come from different machines at different rates, and failures are rare enough that good labels are scarce. I help with data collection and curation, and advise on the experiments that decide which ideas are worth building on.

On the modelling side I compare supervised and self-supervised learning, anomaly detection, multimodal models and foundation models for fault diagnosis. Accuracy is only part of the test. A model also has to hold up on noisy plant data, give an engineer reasoning they can check, and be practical to deploy. The platform itself is built to keep equipment data private.

Fracto was selected among the top 10 teams at the IIM Matrix Startup Program.

Quadruped Stand Stabilization

Developed a terrain-aware PPO policy for Unitree A1 quadruped stand stabilization in MuJoCo using reward shaping, contact-aware constraints, and physics-based simulation.

Designed a 5-stage curriculum learning pipeline over 20M simulation steps, progressively introducing slopes (5°, 15°, 25°), rough terrain, mixed environments, and increasing domain randomization complexity.

Engineered a progressive DR0–DR7 domain randomization framework varying friction (0.5–1.5), motor strength (0.80–1.20), mass (0.93–1.07), actuator noise, control delay, and randomized initial poses for robust policy learning.

Achieved 100% success on flat terrain and 87.5% on slopes, maintaining average stable standing durations of 9.37s and 8.28s; obtained 50% and 37.5% success on rough and stair terrains respectively.

XAIQuest: Seeing Beyond Accuracy

Benchmarked CNN prediction behavior using Grad-CAM, Eigen-CAM, Integrated Gradients, and confidence calibration (ECE = 0.035) to analyze feature attribution, prediction reliability, and model failure modes.

Developed a robustness benchmarking pipeline spanning 8 corruption families and 78 perturbation settings, evaluating resilience under Gaussian/Poisson noise, blur, JPEG compression, brightness, contrast, and color perturbations.

Quantified model degradation under distribution shifts, observing accuracy drops from 89.1% to 21.2% under severe Gaussian noise and 91.0% to 60.9% under color perturbations, highlighting dependence on fine-grained spatial features.

Compared interpretability across multiple CNN architectures, identifying architecture-specific failure patterns and the relationship between confidence calibration and robustness under corrupted inputs.
